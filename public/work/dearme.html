Self-trust, one kept promise at a time.

The problem

Many people call themselves lazy or procrastinators, feel stuck, and start to believe that’s who they are for good. Usually the promise was just too big, or it no longer fits them. Streaks and deadlines then add guilt and shame on top.

Hypothesis

Being stuck is a sign of where it’s hard, not proof you can’t. If people keep promises small enough to win, and see every win pile up in a jar, they will start to trust themselves again.

How it works

No deadlines, no streaks, no overdue, no red marks
  1. 01PromiseMake one to three small promises to yourself. There’s no timeline for when.
  2. 02KeepEach kept promise drops a glowing orb into your jar. A small win is still a win.
  3. 03Shrink itIf a promise is big and hasn’t moved in a while, the optional AI suggests a smaller version, again and again until it’s doable.
  4. 04Refine or releaseIf it no longer fits you, reshape it or let it go. Both are part of trusting yourself.
A small win is a winBig or tiny, a kept promise counts the same. The jar shows what you kept, never what you missed.
No guilt, no timelineNo due dates, no overdue. How long a promise sat untouched is never shown to you.
Refine or releaseChange a promise that no longer fits, or let it go to make space for a new one.
The jar as evidenceTwelve orbs fill a jar, and sealed jars line your shelf: proof that piles up over time.

The AI helper

Optional and off until you turn it on. It looks only at your promise, notices when one is big and hasn’t moved in a while, and asks what makes it hard. Then it suggests a smaller version until you can do it. It never says “you have to”, never shows how long a promise waited, and nothing changes unless you accept it.

The science behind it

Behavioral psychology, with Bandura’s self-efficacy at the center
Bandura, 1977Self-efficacyMastery experiences, doing the thing yourself, are the strongest source of belief that you can.Every kept promise is a mastery experience, and the orb keeps a record of it.
Bandura and Schunk, 1981Micro-commitmentsSmall, near goals built self-efficacy and interest faster than one distant goal.Promises stay small, and the AI shrinks a heavy one into a step you can win.
Weick, 1984; Amabile and Kramer, 2011Small winsVisible progress, even small, keeps motivation going more than anything else at work.The jar makes every small win visible and lets them pile up.
Weiner, 1985Attribution“I’m lazy” is a fixed, personal label. Seeing a cause you can change keeps hope and effort alive.The question is always where it’s hard, never what’s wrong with you.
Wohl, Pychyl and Bennett, 2010Self-forgivenessStudents who forgave themselves for procrastinating procrastinated less before the next exam.No overdue, no red marks, no guilt for a promise that waited.
Wrosch and colleagues, 2003Letting goLetting go of goals that no longer fit, and taking up new ones, was linked to better well-being.Refine and Release are part of self-trust, not failure.

Where it leads

The goal is to not need it. Over time you trust your own word without writing it down, because the evidence is already there. Dear Me is just a start, to help people believe they can.
